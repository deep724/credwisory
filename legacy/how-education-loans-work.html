How education loans work

Understand the journey from planning your studies to receiving funds and starting repayment.

Check your eligibility   Explore loan options

Clear guidance • Compare options • No approval promise

An education loan helps fund your higher studies.

A lender may fund eligible education-related costs and the borrower repays the amount according to agreed loan terms.

Every lender assesses applications differently. Eligibility, interest rates, collateral, documents, and repayment terms can vary.

Collateral-free vs collateral-backed

Collateral-free options

May be available for eligible student profiles. Requirements differ by lender.

Collateral-backed options

May be considered for larger funding needs and can require acceptable security.

Understanding repayment

Moratorium period

Some lenders may offer a period before EMI repayment begins, subject to their terms.

Interest

Interest can accrue based on the lender's loan terms.

EMI

Your monthly repayment depends on the loan amount, rate, and repayment tenure.

Documents you may need

Government-issued identity and address proof • Academic mark sheets and certificates • Admission or offer letter • University fee structure • Passport and visa documents, where applicable • Co-applicant and collateral documents, if applicable.

Frequently asked questions

Is an education loan approval guaranteed?

No. Final decisions are made by the lender.

Can I check eligibility before applying?

Yes. It can help you understand the information that may be considered.

Do all loans require collateral?

No. Requirements vary by lender and applicant profile.

Do I always need a co-applicant?

Requirements vary by lender and applicant profile. A co-applicant may be considered in some cases.

Can loans help cover living expenses?

Some lenders may consider eligible living expenses, subject to their product terms.

What happens after I submit an eligibility enquiry?

Your details can help guide the next conversation; final decisions remain with the lender.

Can I compare more than one lender option?

Comparing relevant routes can be useful because policies and terms may differ.
